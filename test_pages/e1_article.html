Urban gardening on small balconies

Article reference ZQX-E1-ART-2207

Balcony gardens have become popular in dense cities. Even a two-square-metre balcony can grow herbs, cherry tomatoes and lettuce if it gets at least five hours of sun. Containers should have drainage holes, and lightweight potting mix keeps the load on the balcony low.

Watering is the most common problem. Small pots dry out quickly in summer, so many growers use self-watering containers or drip lines on a timer. Mulch on top of the soil also slows evaporation.

Wind is the second problem on high floors. A simple mesh screen protects plants without blocking light. Choosing compact varieties, such as dwarf tomatoes, helps as well.
