**Prompt:** I earn about $250,000 a year. Is it worth paying for a financial advisor?

**Sider:**

Sider Fusion

At $250k income you're in a range where good advice can genuinely pay for itself—but whether it's worth it for you depends less on the income figure and more on how complex your financial life is and how you feel about managing it yourself.

When an advisor is likely worth the cost
If you have a mix of things to juggle—retirement accounts, taxable investments, maybe a home, possibly a business or equity compensation, and questions about when to retire or how to draw down assets—a good advisor earns their fee through tax-aware planning, asset allocation, and keeping you from costly mistakes. At your income level you're often in a bracket where one well-timed tax or contribution decision can cover a year of fees.

When it probably isn't worth it
If your situation is simple—steady paycheck, a work retirement plan, an index-fund portfolio, no debt beyond a mortgage—you can likely do this yourself with a target-date or simple diversified portfolio. The math is straightforward and you don't need to pay 1%+ of assets annually for it.

Watch the fee structure
The cost matters enormously over time. A typical advisor charges around 1% of assets under management, which on a growing portfolio compounds into a substantial sum over decades. Fee-only planners who charge an hourly or flat project fee are often a better fit if you just want a plan checked once, rather than ongoing management.

A middle path
Consider a one-time, fee-only financial plan—pay a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars for a fiduciary to review everything and build you a roadmap. You implement it yourself and revisit every few years. This captures most of the value without the ongoing drag. If you do hire someone, insist they're a fiduciary and be clear about exactly what you're paying and for what.

One thing that would sharpen this a lot: what does your financial life actually look like—are you mostly in a workplace retirement plan and index funds, or is there real complexity (business income, equity, rental property, an inheritance to plan around)?
